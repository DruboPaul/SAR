# Sub-Issue 5: Surface Water Change Detection (Gain/Loss Analysis)

## 1. Seasonal Baseline Dynamics
Our analysis reveals a highly dynamic seasonal cycle across the 11-year baseline:
* **Dry Season (December–February):** Minimum surface water extent (Mean $\approx$ 15,900 $\text{ km}^2$, represented by stable winter baselines).
* **Monsoon Peak (July–August):** Maximum surface water extent (Mean $\approx$ 37,500 $\text{ km}^2$, representing a $\sim$ 2.4-fold seasonal expansion).

## 2. July Monsoon Change Detection (2015 vs 2025)
To understand decadal geomorphological changes and spatial shifts, we performed a change detection analysis comparing the peak monsoon water extent in **July 2015** against **July 2025**:
* **Total Water-Affected Area (July 2025):** 29,360 $\text{ km}^2$
* **Stable Water (Water in both 2015 and 2025):** **15,270.9 $\text{ km}^2$** (representing **52.0%** of the total area).
* **Gained Water (Land in 2015, Water in 2025):** **6,305.0 $\text{ km}^2$** (representing **21.5%** of the total area). This is the source of the claim in the discussion that *“21.5% of the 2025 monsoon water area represents newly gained inundation compared to 2015.”*
* **Lost Water (Water in 2015, Land in 2025):** **7,784.0 $\text{ km}^2$** (representing **26.5%** of the total area).
* **Net Difference:** A net change of $6,305.0 - 7,784.0 = -1,479.0 \text{ km}^2$ on pixel-level, though calibrated national aggregates show July 2015 was 17,331.9 $\text{ km}^2$ and July 2025 was 18,324.8 $\text{ km}^2$.

## 3. Hydrological Interpretation
This spatial distribution confirms that surface water in Bangladesh is undergoing geographical shifts—specifically in the active estuarine zones of the Lower Meghna, major river banks, and the southwestern coastal shrimp farming belt—rather than uniform expansion or contraction. This dynamic is typical of active, highly sedimented deltaic environments.
